CYCLE 01: WHAT WOULD THE EXPERIMENT ACTUALLY MEASURE?
17 October 2026 | Reproduction and preliminary model audit, not a discovery.

The first result is an operational question rather than a new theory: can we
measure a pattern of joint quantum correlations that exceeds what a specified
classical communication model allows, without mistaking another force, a
preparation artifact, or an inappropriate approximation for quantum gravity?

The simple experiment has two masses and four joint paths. Each mass starts in
an equal superposition of left and right. Gravity contributes a different phase
(a quantum wave's angle) to LL, LR, RL and RR. Only a joint phase combination,
Delta = phi_LL + phi_RR - phi_LR - phi_RL, can entangle this ideal initial state.
A phase belonging to A alone, B alone, or all branches together cancels out.
Merely counting the four paths would see 25% each even when the state entangles.
Sources S01, S16–S19 establish the underlying framework; our code reproduces the
static, finite-dimensional calculation, not an entire interferometer.

A WORKED NUMERICAL EXAMPLE
Take two 10^-14 kg point masses, a centre separation of 450 micrometres, two
paths per mass separated by 250 micrometres, and a 2.5-second static hold. These
are illustrative assumptions, not equipment specifications we have achieved.
The branch separations are 450, 700, 200 and 450 micrometres. The calculated
joint phase is -0.313934 radians, concurrence 0.156323 and negativity 0.078162.
Concurrence ranges from 0 to 1; negativity for two qubits ranges from 0 to 0.5.
Both measure entanglement here, with different normalization.

A THREE-SETTING READOUT
After calibrated local rotations, use fresh mass pairs to measure XX, YY and ZZ
spin correlations. X, Y and Z label three perpendicular spin measurement axes,
not three different gravitational forces. Each correlation is the average of
the product of two outcomes, each +1 or -1. Form S = <XX> - <YY> + <ZZ>.
Every separable two-qubit state has S <= 1 under the trusted-measurement
assumptions. This is an entanglement witness, not a Bell/device-independent test.

For the example above, suppose ordinary independent dephasing retains 95% of
each branch coherence. The calculated S is 1.199515, with negativity 0.049879.
The equal-noise family loses detectable entanglement at retention 0.855821.
95% is an assumption to test, not an experimental achievement. The package
contains every four-outcome probability for all three settings and a conditional
sampling bound. This does not establish instrument fidelity or running time.

FIRST EASY OPPONENT, THEN A HARDER ONE
The projected Kafri–Taylor–Milburn classical-channel benchmark ties force to
sufficient decoherence to keep our initially separable masses unentangled (S02).
We reproduced that behavior and its analytic boundary. It is not the strongest
currently viable competitor: particular pairwise extensions have already faced
experimental constraints (S21).

We also implemented the Gaussian-smeared Diósi–Penrose dynamics analyzed by
Trillo and Navascués (S05). Our computation reproduces their two limiting distance
ratios, 0.850872 and 2.210928, and an explicit case that DOES entangle. The code
uses both the direct solution and a independently reconstructed Lindblad
propagator. Thus our framework now includes a mathematically harder opponent.
That does not establish the model's classical interpretation or physical
viability. The example's cutoff length and particle geometry must face existing
constraints. Aspelmeyer's 2026 paper argues actual table-top constraints change
the collapse-model conclusion (S20); that is the next mandatory audit.

TWO TRAPS WE CAN NOW CATCH
First, an approximation that assumes path splitting is much smaller than the
centre separation underestimates the phase by 30.9% in our illustrative geometry.
Using exact gravity for one model and this approximation for another could
manufacture a model difference. At a splitting/separation ratio of 0.1, the
error is 1%. We must match approximation regimes before comparing theories.

Second, lost single-particle interference is not automatically environmental
decoherence. Entanglement itself can remove it. In this family the visibility is
lambda * |cos(Delta/2)|, not lambda. At phase pi it vanishes even without noise.
A noise-control experiment must therefore isolate the noise channel rather than
read off its strength from a single final visibility measurement.

A CORRECTION TO THE INITIAL CHARTER
The Aziz–Howl Nature result was presented too confidently. Direct rebuttals
contest its dynamics and/or attribute its apparent entanglement to quantum matter
exchange, not a classical gravitational mediator (S04, S14, S15, S22). The
conditional local-mediator witness has not simply been overturned. We must keep
the mathematical prediction, subsystem choice, mediation assumptions and
experimental inference separate. These full QFT arguments are not yet reproduced.

WHAT IS COMPLETE
970 numerical/property/unit checks pass. Known formulas, selected classical-
channel behavior and the harder DP example are reproduced. Five reproducible
figures and source/claim registries are saved. There is a first operational
readout, but no complete robust discriminator against all viable alternatives.

WHAT IS NOT COMPLETE
No apparatus model, new experiment, full viability scan, novelty claim, external
physics review, or Lean-kernel verification. GitHub remote creation is blocked by
the available connector action set; the local Git package preserves the work.
The next gate is to determine which harder models survive physical constraints,
then compare full readout distributions in the SAME realizable geometry.
